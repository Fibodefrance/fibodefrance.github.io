Fishing Logbook

Privacy Policy

Applies to the game Fishing Logbook on iPhone and iPad, all versions. Last updated: November 9, 2026.

In short

Fishing Logbook has no account and no sign-up. It sends its publisher game statistics with no name and no account, which you can turn off, and, for the weekly contest, the size of your catches under a randomly drawn name. Ads are optional and provided by Google: none is shown unless you choose to watch it. Your logbook stays on your device, and a copy goes to your own iCloud account. Nothing is sold.

The game identifier does not identify a person or a device. It is a number drawn at random by the game the first time it opens. It does not come from any identifier of your device, your Apple account or advertising. Deleting the game deletes it.

The time of day, the season and the hemisphere come from your device's clock and region, read on the device: the game never asks for your location. The in-game weather is the same for everyone, drawn from the date.

What leaves the device

The iCloud backup

If your iPhone or iPad is signed in to iCloud, a copy of your logbook is stored in iCloud, on your Apple account, so you can find it again on another device or after reinstalling. It is your space, not ours: we have no access to it. If two different logbooks exist (two devices), the game asks you which one to keep, and the other one is set aside on the device: nothing is overwritten without asking you. To delete this copy: Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Storage.

Game statistics

To find out whether players come back, and where the game loses them, Fishing Logbook sends our server a few events, with no name and no account: the first launch, each day you open the game (once a day), the time of day you fish (dawn, daytime, dusk or night, once a day), steps of the game (the first catch, the number of species found, a place opened, a legendary fish, the first fish kept or released, the first gear bought with coins, the first letter, the first object found, the first contest catch, the first missed bite, the first fish that got away, opening the shop, a purchase — without its amount —, an ad watched, reminders turned on, a logbook restored from iCloud) and a few play times (2, 5, 10, 20 and 40 minutes). Each one carries the number of days since installation and its date, the platform, the game version and its language. When offline, they wait in your game and are sent later.

What links them: the randomly drawn game identifier (see above). Nothing else: no name, no contact details, no location, no device model, no IP address in our tables. We only read these statistics grouped together, by day of installation ("how many come back the next day?"), and cannot find yours. Because one identifier links your events together, they are not anonymous but pseudonymous.

Where and for how long: at Supabase (servers in Ireland, in the European Union), for 13 months, after which the database deletes them automatically.

Why: to improve the game, on the basis of its publisher's legitimate interest in knowing whether people enjoy it. None of it is sold or shared, and none of it is used for advertising.

Turning them off: the logbook → Settings → Statistics: "Off". When off, nothing more is sent, and whatever was waiting in your game is deleted.

The weekly contest

When you catch the fish of the week, the game sends our server the game identifier, the week, the fish, its size, and your name on the board. This name is never free text: it is three numbers (an animal, a word, a number) drawn at random, which each phone writes in its own language, and which you can change. The board shows other players this name and the size of your finest catch, nothing else. The server also tells the game which week it is. This data is kept at Supabase (Ireland) for 13 months, then deleted automatically. It is only used to run the contest.

In-app purchases

They go through Apple, which takes the payment: we receive neither your name, nor your contact details, nor any payment data. The game keeps the number of each delivered purchase on the device, so it is never delivered twice.

Reminders

The two reminders (the full moon, when evening comes; the end of the contest, if you have a catch in it) are off by default: you turn them on if you want, and iOS then asks for your permission. They are notifications scheduled on the device itself: nothing goes through a server.

Advertising

Fishing Logbook offers optional rewarded ads (doubling the coins for a fish you release, or the bait of the day), provided by Google AdMob. None opens on its own: you choose them by tapping their button. There are no banners and no ads between screens.

Your consent, where the law requires it (European Union, United Kingdom, Switzerland, some US states): Google's window asks for it the first time you tap an ad. At each launch, Google's kit checks this choice and, if it allows, prepares an ad in advance.

What Google collects to show an ad, measure it and fight fraud: the IP address (and from it an approximate location), information about the device and the app, interactions with the ad, and diagnostic data. Ads are requested non-personalised, and the game does not ask for Apple's tracking permission: it has no access to the advertising identifier. Google processes this data under its own policy: policies.google.com/privacy.

Changing your choices: where the law provides for it, a "My ad choices" button appears in the shop; it reopens Google's window.

Children

The game is not specifically aimed at children and asks them for no information. It contains in-app purchases: Screen Time on iPhone and iPad can block them.

Your rights

The European General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) gives you rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability over your personal data.

  • Game statistics and the contest carry no name, no contact details and no account, and we do not know which ones are yours: we can therefore neither give them to you nor delete them. To stop sending statistics, turn them off in the game's settings; everything already sent is deleted automatically after 13 months.
  • Your logbook: deleting the game deletes it from the device; the iCloud copy is deleted in iCloud settings.
  • Advertising data: change your choices in Google's window; your rights are exercised with Google, at myaccount.google.com.

You can also write to us at any time at legionduparadis@gmail.com; we reply within 5 working days. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with the CNIL, the French data protection authority.

Changes

Any change to this policy will be published on this page, with its date. A change in what the game transmits would be announced in the game before taking effect.
